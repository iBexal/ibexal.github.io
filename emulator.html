Spectrum emulator

A neural network that stands in for a stellar spectral synthesis code, running live in your browser.

I fit low signal-to-noise spectra of stars in globular clusters and stellar streams observed with the AAOmega spectrograph on the Anglo-Australian Telescope. Its red arm covers the infrared Ca triplet, 8440 - 8785 Å, at a resolving power of about 10,500. Fitting one spectrum properly can take hundres of model spectra and many time-expensive iterations, so I train an emulator once and fit quickly with that.

Move the sliders to change the star. Lower K to keep only the first principal components and see how much of the spectrum they carry; the bars show the network's outputs, one per component. This is work in progress: the emulator is still being validated.

What to look for

  • The three Ca II triplet lines (dashed lines at 8498, 8542, and 8662 Å) dominate. They strengthen with metallicity and with [α/Fe], which in this model sets the calcium abundance.
  • Untie [Mg/Fe], [Si/Fe] and [Ti/Fe] to move their lines on their own, for example the Si I lines near 8728, 8742, and 8752 Å.
  • In hotter stars the hydrogen Paschen lines appear, such as P12 at 8750 Å.
  • Ten principal components already reproduce the spectrum to about 0.4% of the continuum; all 111 reach 0.01%.

Spectral synthesis: Korg (Wheeler et al. 2023). Model atmospheres: MARCS (Gustafsson et al. 2008). Line list: Gaia-ESO (Heiter et al. 2021), recalibrated on the Sun and Arcturus.
