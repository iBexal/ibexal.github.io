<h1 id="research">My Research</h1>

<h2 id="cannon-project" class="research-topic">Maximising the Scientific Returns of Spectroscopic Surveys Using Machine Learning</h2>
<p>
My current research uses data-driven models to extract stellar labels from high-resolution spectroscopic surveys, and applies them to low-resolution, low signal-to-noise spectra.
</p>

<div class="timeline-item">
<h3>Goals</h3>
<ul>
    <li>Improve the precision of stellar parameters</li>
    <li>Measure chemical abundances</li>
    <li>Make better use of archival spectra</li>
    <li>Investigate the structure and formation history of the Milky Way</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="timeline-item">
<h3>Data</h3>
<p>
I am using AAOmega archival spectra, and AAOmega spectra taken for the S5 survey:
</p>
<ul>
    <li>Focus on 1700D and 580V gratings</li>
    <li>Large samples of Globular Cluster, Dwarf Galaxy and Stellar Stream stars</li>
</ul>
<p>
I am using labels from higher resolution southern hemisphere spectroscopic stellar surveys:
</p>
<ul>
    <li>GALAH</li>
    <li>APOGEE</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="timeline-item">
<h3>Methods</h3>
<p>
I use the data-driven algorithm <i>The Cannon</i> to transfer information from well-characterised training labels from spectroscopic surveys to archival AAOmega spectra.
</p>
</div>

<h2 id="exoplanets" class="research-topic">Distinguishing the Chemical Abundances of Exoplanet Host Stars</h2>
<p>
I use spectral synthesis to derive stellar parameters and chemical abundances for exoplanet hosting stars.
</p>
<div class="timeline-item">
    <h3>Methods</h3>
    <p>
        I synthesise stellar spectra using <TT>MOOG</TT> via <i>iSpec</i> to derive stellar parameters, and 9 chemical abundances for 103 exoplanet hosts.
    </p>
</div>
<div class="timeline-item">
    <h3>Results</h3>
    <p>
        I replicate the known relationship between stellar host metallicity, and total mass of hosted planets. I find that stars that host at least one planet, but do not host any planets of mass less than 30 Earth masses have statistically different chemical signatures to those that do. <a href="/publications#exoplanet-paper">Read the paper here</a>.
    </p>
</div>
<hr>

<h2 id="tsne" class="research-topic">Extracting Milky Way Stellar Populations Using t-SNE</h2>

I apply the statistical method t-SNE (t-distributed Stochastic Neighbour Embedding) and clustering algorithm OPTICS to GALAH+ DR3 chemical abundances to distinguish the Milky Way high-alph disk, low-alpha disk, and the Galactic stellar halo. t-SNE finds populations in the Milky Way using only GALAH chemical abundances, that are kinematically consistent. <a href="/publications#tsne-proceeding">Read the proceedings here</a>.
