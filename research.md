<h2 id="cannon-project">Maximising the Scientific Returns of Spectroscopic Surveys Using Machine Learning</h2>

My current research uses data-driven models to extract stellar labels from high-resolution spectroscopic surveys, and applies them to low-resolution, low signal-to-noise spectra.

### Goals

- Improve the precision of stellar parameters
- Measure chemical abundances
- Make better use of archival spectra
- Investigate the structure and formation history of the Milky Way

### Data

I am using AAOmega archival spectra, and AAOmega spectra taken for the S5 survey:

- Focus on 1700D and 580V gratings
- Large samples of Globular Cluster, Dwarf Galaxy and Stellar Stream stars

I am using labels from higher resolution southern hemisphere spectroscopic stellar surveys:

- GALAH
- APOGEE

### Methods

I use the data-driven algorithm *The Cannon* to transfer information from well-characterised training labels from spectroscopic surveys to archival AAOmega spectra.

<h2 id="exoplanets">Distinguishing the Chemical Abundances of Exoplanet Host Stars</h2>

I use spectral synthesis to derive stellar parameters and chemical abundances for exoplanet hosting stars.

<div class="timeline-item">
    <h3>Methods</h3>
    <p>
        I synthesise stellar spectra using <TT>MOOG</TT> via <i>iSpec</i> to derive stellar parameters, and 9 chemical abundances for 103 exoplanet hosts.
    </p>
</div>
<div class="timeline-item">
    <h3>Results</h3>
    <p>
        I replicate the known relationship between stellar host metallicity, and total mass of hosted planets
    </p>
    <p>
        I find that stars that host at least one planet, but do not host any planets of mass less than 30 Earth mass have different chemical signatures to those that do.
    </p>
</div>
